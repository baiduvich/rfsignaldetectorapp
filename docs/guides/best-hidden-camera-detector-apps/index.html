1. RF Signal Detector, the most complete sweep on iPhone

Full disclosure: this is our app, so read this entry as a statement of what it does rather than an impartial review, and weigh the rest of this list accordingly.

What earns RF Signal Detector the top spot on our own site is coverage. Most detector apps do one thing. This one runs the three techniques that actually work on a phone, in sequence, and then tells you what to do with the results. The Bluetooth scanner finds and identifies nearby Bluetooth devices and trackers, including the item trackers increasingly used for stalking. The Wi-Fi scanner lists every device connected to the local network, which is how most consumer spy cameras get caught, because they have to join the same Wi-Fi you are on to stream footage. The infrared lens detector uses your iPhone camera to spot the IR illuminators that night-vision cameras use, which are invisible to your eyes but not to a camera sensor.

2. Fing, the best free network scanner

Fing is the app most often recommended by mainstream tech press, and the recommendation is deserved. It scans the Wi-Fi network you are connected to and lists every device on it, and its device identification is the best in the category. Where other scanners show you a bare MAC address, Fing can often name the manufacturer and device type, so an entry like "Wyze Cam" or a camera-brand chipset jumps out immediately.

The core network scan is free, with paid upgrades for deeper monitoring features. What Fing does not do is optical detection. It cannot help you find a camera that records locally without joining the network, and it has no lens or infrared mode. Use it as the network half of a sweep and pair it with a lens check. If you are scanning a rental, our walkthrough on finding hidden cameras in an Airbnb shows exactly where network results fit in the process.

3. Hidden Camera Detector by FutureApps, the Android veteran

This is one of the oldest and most downloaded dedicated detector apps, and on Android it remains a strong pick. It offers three detection approaches: an AI-assisted lens detector that uses the camera to spot pinhole and CCTV lenses, an infrared mode for night-vision cameras, and a magnetometer-based scan on Android that reacts to the magnetic fields of powered electronics. The magnetometer feature is the weakest of the three, since speakers, wiring, and metal studs trigger it constantly, but the lens and infrared modes are genuinely useful.

Pricing differs by platform: the Android version is free with upgrades, while the iOS version is sold as a one-time purchase rather than a subscription, which some buyers will prefer. The interface shows its age, and the free tier's Wi-Fi scanning is limited, but the underlying detection is real.

4. Ubiquiti WiFiman, the free network audit tool

WiFiman is not marketed as a spy camera app at all, and that is part of its appeal. It is a free, ad-free network utility from Ubiquiti, a serious networking company, and its Discovery tab enumerates every device on the subnet using multiple discovery protocols, showing IP addresses, manufacturers, and other identifying details. There is no upsell, no fake radar animation, and no scare tactics.

The tradeoff is that it assumes some comfort with networking. It will hand you an accurate device list but will not hold your hand interpreting it, and it has no camera-specific features, no lens detection, and no sweep guidance. If you already know how to read a device list, it is arguably the cleanest free tool in this space. If you do not, paste your scan results into our free hidden camera device checker to find out whether a mystery device name belongs to a known camera brand.

5. Hidden Camera Detector - Peek, quick lens checks on iOS

Peek is a popular iPhone option focused on optical detection, using the camera to help you spot lens reflections, alongside network scanning. It is easy to pick up and the lens-checking flow is straightforward, which makes it a reasonable choice for a fast once-over of a hotel room.

The caveats: independent testers have found its accuracy inconsistent, and the free tier is limited, with results gated behind a weekly subscription. It can handle a quick check, but read the subscription terms before tapping through, and do not treat a clean Peek scan as a complete sweep.

6. Glint Finder, the free optical option for Android

Glint Finder is an Android app built around a single technique: retroreflection. Camera lenses bounce light straight back at its source, so if you shine your phone flash around a dark room and watch the screen, a hidden lens shows up as a bright, persistent pinpoint glint. Glint Finder automates that flash-and-look loop.

Because it does one simple thing, it is free, light, and honest about its scope. Expect false positives from screws, glass, and glossy plastic; move slowly, keep the room dark, and check any glint from multiple angles. A real lens keeps glinting straight back at you. It is a solid free companion to a network scanner for Android users.

7. NoSpy, the multi-mode newcomer

NoSpy has been getting attention in 2026 roundups for running several detection modes together: an AI model trained to recognize camera lens shapes, infrared detection, a magnetic field scanner, and a Wi-Fi network audit. In one published lab test from May 2026 it flagged the large majority of planted camera placements, a strong result for a phone app, though as with any vendor-adjacent testing you should treat exact percentages with healthy skepticism.

It is a newer app with a shorter track record than Fing or FutureApps, and full functionality sits behind paid upgrades. But the multi-method approach is the right architecture, and it is worth a look for travelers who want an all-in-one scanner and are not on iOS with our app already installed.

What no app can detect

Any list of the best hidden camera detector apps is incomplete without this section, because every app on this page shares the same blind spots.

Wired cameras that record locally. A camera hardwired for power that writes to a memory card emits no Wi-Fi, no Bluetooth, and joins no network. Network scanners are blind to it by definition. Your only phone-based tool against it is optical: the lens reflection and infrared checks, done carefully in darkness.

Cellular devices. A camera or GPS bug with its own SIM card transmits on cellular bands that phone hardware cannot scan for you. Apps that claim to "detect RF" in this sense are overpromising; consumer phones do not expose raw spectrum data. Detecting cellular bugs requires dedicated RF hardware.

Anything powered off. A device on a timer or motion trigger may be silent during your scan and active an hour later. This is why a physical inspection of common hiding spots always belongs in a sweep, whatever app you use. Audio bugs have their own quirks too, covered in our guide to detecting listening devices.

If your situation involves a stalker, an ex-partner with access to your home, or a legal dispute, do not rely on any app. Hire a TSCM (technical surveillance countermeasures) professional and involve law enforcement.

How we evaluated these apps

We ranked apps on four questions. First, does the detection method have a real physical basis? Wi-Fi enumeration, Bluetooth scanning, IR detection through a camera sensor, and lens retroreflection all do; vague "radiation detectors" do not. Second, how much of the problem does the app cover? Multi-method apps ranked above single-trick apps. Third, is the pricing honest? We noted free tiers that gate core results behind aggressive subscriptions. Fourth, does the app help you act on results, with device identification or sweep guidance, rather than just showing a scary graph? Facts about competing apps come from their store listings, publisher documentation, and independent 2026 coverage; where we could not verify an exact price, we used general phrasing rather than guessing. And to restate the obvious bias: RF Signal Detector is our own app, which is why we disclosed it up front.

Frequently asked questions

Do hidden camera detector apps actually work?

Yes, for the right camera types. Apps are genuinely effective against wireless cameras that connect over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth, and lens detection can catch cameras that transmit nothing at all. No app can detect a wired camera that records locally and emits no light, so treat apps as a strong first pass, not a guarantee.

What is the best hidden camera detector app for iPhone?

RF Signal Detector is our pick for iPhone because it combines Bluetooth scanning, full Wi-Fi network scanning, an infrared lens detector, and a guided room sweep in one app. Full disclosure, it is our app. Fing and Ubiquiti WiFiman are excellent free network-only alternatives.

Can a free app find hidden cameras?

Yes. Fing and Ubiquiti WiFiman are free and will list every device on the Wi-Fi network, which exposes many consumer spy cameras. Free tiers of dedicated detector apps are often limited, so expect to pay or start a trial for lens detection and full results in most of them.

What can no hidden camera detector app find?

No phone app can detect a wired camera that records to a local memory card and never transmits, a camera on a separate cellular connection, or a device that is powered off during your scan. For those you need a physical search, a lens reflection check in darkness, and in high-stakes cases professional TSCM equipment.
